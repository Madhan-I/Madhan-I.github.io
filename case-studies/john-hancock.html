Case study

Restarting a frozen platform

John Hancock · AWD to SS&C Chorus BPM · Sep 2022 – Aug 2025 · Boston, on-site

  • 400+backlog tickets cleared in 9 months
  • 20+business divisions served
  • 5modernization initiatives taken live
  • ±10%estimate accuracy within the first quarter

The situation

John Hancock's AWD workflow platform hadn't been upgraded since 2019. COVID froze the program for two years, leaving 400+ tickets waiting, and more than 20 business divisions depended on the platform.

First move: agree what "done" meant

Before touching the backlog, we sat down with the client to settle one question: clear the old tickets first, or modernize the platform? Picking one direction up front meant the team wasn't pulled two ways at once.

Handling competing priorities

Every business stakeholder wanted their tickets handled first. Instead of negotiating one by one, I brought all of them into a single session. We weighted every request by business criticality and came out with one ranked backlog that everyone had agreed to.

Fixing how work moved

I set up release governance and Function Point capacity planning. Within the first quarter, effort estimates were within 10% of actual work, and overtime and idle time both dropped.

Keeping the client close

I held a check-in with our primary client contact every two-week sprint, so they saw each step as it happened rather than at the end.

Results

  • 400+ backlog tickets cleared in 9 months
  • First AWD upgrades since 2019, with most of the work done by Q2 2023
  • 5 modernization initiatives taken live across 20+ business divisions
  • All teams moved off the legacy EnCorr and Viewstation desktops onto SS&C Chorus BPM before handover in Aug 2025

What I'd repeat on every program

Ranking priorities with every stakeholder in one room, in the first weeks, prevented months of escalations later. I now run that weighting session in week one of every program I lead.
