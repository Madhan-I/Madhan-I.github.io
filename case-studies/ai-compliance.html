Taking a compliance AI engine to production

Global insurer · Core API, Customer Engagement & Compliance AI · Sep 2025 – Present · Hybrid

  • 30% → 10–15%false alarms in customer onboarding
  • 95%+average ART predictability, no committed work carried over
  • 14+teams and 150+ people across three products
  • <5%sprint variance, with a High portfolio health rating

The situation

In September 2025 I took over governance for three products at a global insurer: Core API, Customer Engagement and Compliance AI. Onboarding checks were flagging around 30% of new customers as possible issues. Most weren't, and every one of those false alarms meant a person had to stop, review and clear it.

The approach the team took

The engineers built a hybrid engine: rules where rules work well, and an AI layer that uses retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to bring the right policy into each decision. A person still checks every case the AI flags. My job was to give that work direction and ship it without putting the rest of the portfolio at risk.

Keeping the rest of the portfolio steady

The other products didn't pause while this happened. In the same period we moved 15 core services onto Azure Kubernetes Service and 7 microservices onto Gradle, and strengthened platform security. Any one of those can derail a quarter, so I kept PI planning honest about capacity, raised risks early and reported portfolio health and roadmap decisions straight to executive leadership.

Staying close to the teams

After every sprint I sit down with each Scrum Master to hear what's working, what isn't and what they need from me. I also run regular sessions on SAFe updates, so the whole train keeps up with how the framework is changing.

Results

  • Onboarding false alarms down from 30% to 10–15%, cutting manual compliance review
  • 95%+ average ART predictability (business value delivered against business value planned each PI), with no committed work carried over
  • A sustained High health rating across the products, with under 5% sprint variance

What I'd repeat on every AI program

AI work is still delivery work. The model matters, but so does everything around it: a clear definition of done, a team that can say "not this sprint", and a portfolio view that shows leadership where the risk really sits.
