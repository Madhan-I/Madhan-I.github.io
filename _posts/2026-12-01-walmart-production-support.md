Everyone remembers their first job in tech. Mine, from July 2014 to June 2015, was production support at Cognizant for Walmart's Item File, Order Management System and the legacy platforms around them.

Production support teaches you something no classroom does: systems fail at the worst possible moment, and someone has to be ready.

## Under pressure

During my year on the team I helped resolve **more than 50 critical P1 and P2 outages** and **120 high-priority incidents**, including during peak retail periods when uptime matters most. We consistently beat our SLA targets and earned client commendations for keeping systems running.

## Fixing the causes, not just the symptoms

Firefighting is necessary. But the real wins came from reducing how often we had to fight fires at all.

- **Automation.** I automated the competitive ("Comp") reporting in the Order Management System, cutting manual effort by 30% and saving **25 engineering hours every week**.
- **Performance.** Working with the database team to redesign query execution, we improved Supplier Volume Inventory Sales Tracking (SVIST) reporting performance by **200%**, which shortened supplier response times.
- **Cost.** Operational efficiency measures across the platform reduced production maintenance costs by **17% within five months**.
- **Speed of recovery.** We reduced incident resolution time for the Walmart Item File system by **20% within six months**.

And through all of it, we kept a **100% on-time milestone record** every quarter.

## Why it still matters to me

Today I run release trains and portfolios. But I still think like someone who has worked production support. Every release decision I make carries a quiet question from those days: what happens to the people on support if this goes wrong?

That question has saved more projects than any framework.
