On August 5, 2025, a few weeks before I left Boston, I went to Adobe Create Now Boston. It was a free evening event at Artists For Humanity in South Boston, a nonprofit that employs young people in the arts. Adobe's team demonstrated the latest updates to Photoshop, Illustrator and Adobe Express, with Firefly, Adobe's generative AI, running through all of them. Local creatives shared their own workflows, and the rest of the evening was for meeting people.

## From headline to everyday tool

Generative AI often arrives as a headline. What struck me that evening was how ordinary Adobe was making it look. Firefly wasn't a separate product to learn; it sat inside tools millions of people already use every day. You select something, describe what you want, and keep working.

That's the difference between a demo and adoption. People don't change how they work because a feature exists. They change when it fits into what they already do.

## What I took away

**Put AI where the work already happens.** The value of a feature like this is in the tedious steps it removes from a job people already do. That's the test I now use for any AI feature on a roadmap I run.

**Real users explain value better than vendors.** The local creatives showing their own workflows did more to explain the tools than any slide could. When I plan an AI rollout, the best people to show it off are early users, not the delivery team.

**Trust comes before scale.** Adobe has made a point of training Firefly on licensed and public-domain content so businesses can use its output commercially. In regulated industries like the insurance work I deliver, that kind of question comes first, not last.

## The people

The evening was relaxed, with food, drinks and plenty of conversation between designers, marketers and technologists. I left with more than notes on new features: I made good friends that night, people I'm still in touch with today.

## Why it matters for my day job

I lead delivery on AI-enabled platforms, including a compliance engine now in production. Seeing a company like Adobe build generative AI into everyday tools sharpened how I think about adoption: fit the work, prove the value with real users, and earn trust early.
