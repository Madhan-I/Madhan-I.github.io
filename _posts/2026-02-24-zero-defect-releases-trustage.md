In January 2020 I joined Syntel, part of Atos, as Scrum Master and Agile Delivery Lead for TruStage Financial Group, then known as CUNA Mutual Group.

We were a new team joining an established project. Within two months we were at full speed, completing half of the work on the xPression document project with a third of the existing vendor's headcount.

## The work

We delivered customer documents on OpenText xPression, managing design and delivery through xAdmin and xDashboard. Over eight months the team completed **more than 2,000 document requests**.

Volume like that creates a familiar risk: the faster you go, the more likely a mistake reaches production. In financial services, a wrong document isn't a cosmetic issue. It reaches a member's mailbox.

## Quality as a system, not a step

Rather than adding more manual checking at the end, we built quality into the flow with a **multi-tier automated testing framework**:

- **Regression tests**, so new changes didn't break old documents
- **Smoke tests**, so every build was sanity-checked before anyone spent time on it
- **Schema verification**, so data problems were caught before they became document problems

Automation took the repetitive checking off people and gave them back time for the reviews that need human judgment.

## Working across time zones

The team spanned on-site and offshore locations. I mentored both sides and led sprint retrospectives that focused on one question: what slowed us down this sprint, and what will we change next sprint?

## The outcome

- A new team at **full speed within two months**, doing **half the work with a third of the headcount**.
- **2,000+ document requests** completed.
- **Zero-defect production releases.**
- Project schedules **reduced by 35%**.

## The lesson

Speed and quality aren't opposites. When testing is automated and built into the flow, quality is what makes speed possible. Every clean release builds the trust that lets the next one go faster.
