From July 2015 to June 2017 I worked at Cognizant as a Systems Engineer and Application Analyst for Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield (now Elevance Health), as part of a cross-functional team of 60 developers.

## Too many versions of the same thing

Over the years, document layouts had multiplied. Small variations became separate templates, and every separate template had to be maintained, tested and fixed on its own. Nobody planned it that way. It just accumulated.

That accumulation had a real cost: more effort for every change, more places for defects to appear, and slower turnaround for the business.

## Consolidating to master templates

Using OpenText Exstream and xPression, we consolidated legacy layouts into **dynamic master templates**. One well-built template could now do the work of many, adapting to the content it needed to carry.

We also worked on what happened after release. Defects found after implementation are the most expensive kind, so we introduced **automated pre-flight QA checklists** and standardized knowledge transfer so that fixes and lessons didn't depend on who happened to be on shift.

## The results

- Operating expenses **reduced by $300,000 a year**.
- Contributed to a **$400,000 annual revenue increase** through improved client retention.
- Bug resolution cycles **improved by 15%** across the 60-developer team.
- Post-implementation defects **cut by 25%**.

## The takeaway

Complexity is rarely added on purpose. It creeps in one sensible exception at a time. Every so often, someone has to step back and ask: how many of these really need to exist? At Anthem, the answer saved real money and gave the team time back for the work that mattered.

*If your systems have grown more complex than your business needs, I'd enjoy helping simplify them.*
