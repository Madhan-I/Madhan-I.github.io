Some programmes are big because of the technology. The Allstate programme I led from September 2020 to August 2022 was big because of its audience.

Every document our team shipped went out to **more than 20 million Allstate and 400,000 Encompass policyholders**. Bills, notices, policy letters, the communications people actually read. Across the programme we deployed **more than 750 document templates**. When something is wrong at that scale, it is wrong for millions of people at once.

## The pressure point

Releases were getting stuck behind blockers that lasted weeks, sitting between engineering and the client. The goal was to deliver more and deliver on time, without adding headcount.

So instead of adding people, we changed how the team worked.

## What we changed

As Senior Scrum Master and Agile Project Manager, I focused on two things.

**Coaching the team, not just running ceremonies.** We tightened how work was refined, sized and pulled, and improved the parts of the delivery lifecycle where work was waiting rather than moving.

**Owning escalations.** I became the primary escalation point between engineering and client executives. Release blockers stopped bouncing between teams because there was one person responsible for clearing them. That's what let us move production deployments from monthly to near-weekly.

## The results

- Delivery velocity **up 30%**.
- Programme cost **down 25%**.
- **No increase in headcount.**
- Production releases moved from **monthly to near-weekly**.

## Why it stuck

The gains lasted because they came from the system, not from heroics. Nobody was working nights to hit those numbers. The team was simply spending more of its time on work that moved, and less on work that waited.

That's the part of Scrum I care about most: not the meetings, but the flow they're meant to protect.
