On May 4, 2023, I went to Kresge Auditorium on the MIT campus for the opening keynote session of Solve at MIT 2023. MIT Solve is an MIT initiative that runs open challenges to find tech-based solutions to urgent global problems, then backs the social entrepreneurs behind them, called Solver teams. Its annual event brings these teams together with funders and leaders for three days. The opening session, where all attendees gather for the keynote talks, is the part that's open to the public.

## The session

The session was called "Unlocking our Creative Potential". Its theme was how technology can widen access and opportunity, so that talent anywhere gets the chance to flourish. Speakers included MIT President Sally Kornbluth and Tony Elumelu, founder and chairman of Heirs Holdings, alongside entrepreneurs, advocates and artists.

## What stayed with me

**Technology is the easy part.** The people on stage weren't really talking about tools. They talked about access: who gets the chance to use technology, learn it and build with it. That's the same question I care about when I train graduates and students.

**Impact needs delivery.** Every idea on that stage still needed someone to turn it into a plan, line up partners and keep it moving. That's the part of the work I know best, and it was good to see how much it matters outside the corporate world.

**Talent is everywhere.** I've trained more than 3,000 people since 2020, many of them early in their careers. Hearing global leaders say what I see in every classroom, that talent is everywhere but opportunity isn't, was a quiet confirmation of why I keep teaching.

## The people

Events like this are as much about the room as the stage. I got talking with people from very different backgrounds, and some of those conversations turned into friendships that have lasted well beyond that afternoon. We're still in touch today.

## Why it matters for my day job

Delivery is how good intentions become real outcomes. Solve at MIT was a reminder that the skills I use to run release trains also matter wherever people are trying to make technology work for more of the world.
