In 2022 I was on-site in Boston for John Hancock, restarting a platform programme that had been frozen for two years. In the evenings I joined Code for Boston, a volunteer group that builds free, open-source tools for public-service organisations. Its weekly hack nights met at the Cambridge Innovation Center, at 1 Broadway in Cambridge.

I didn't go there to write code. I did the same job I do at work: coordinating volunteers and mentoring developers so a team could actually finish what it started.

## Two projects

**Urban League Heat Pump app.** In August 2022 Code for Boston started a project with the Urban League of Eastern Massachusetts and the Heat Smart Alliance to speed up heat pump adoption in the Boston area, with a focus on equity. The app brings in property data and helps outreach workers decide where to focus their effort. Heat pumps are part of the Massachusetts Clean Energy and Climate Plan, so it was work with a clear public purpose.

**National Police Data Coalition.** In spring 2023 the group restarted this project. It's an open repository of independently collected police-conduct data, gathered from journalists, attorneys and community members, for researchers, lawyers and policymakers. A restart means old code, lost context and new people, and I'd seen that pattern before.

## What volunteer teams teach you

**Nobody has to show up.** At work, a sprint plan comes with an employment contract behind it. On a hack night it doesn't. People come because the work matters and because the next step is clear. If the next step isn't clear, they don't come back.

**Clarity beats authority.** Small tasks that someone can finish in an evening, a backlog anyone can read, and the reason behind the work said out loud: those did more than any deadline could.

**Mentoring is the multiplier.** A newer developer who gets unstuck in their first session comes back. One who spends the night fighting setup usually doesn't.

**Restarts follow the same rules everywhere.** Reviving a stalled volunteer project felt a lot like reviving a stalled enterprise programme: recover the context, shrink the first steps, and make progress visible quickly.

## Why it matters for my day job

A release train is a team of teams, and many people on it don't report to the person running it. Volunteering sharpened the skill that matters most there: leading through clarity and trust instead of a title.
