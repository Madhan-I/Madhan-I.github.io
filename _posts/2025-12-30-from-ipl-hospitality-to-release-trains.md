People are sometimes surprised that my career didn't start in technology. Alongside my Electrical and Electronics Engineering degree, I worked in live events. I've come to think it was some of the best preparation I could have had.

## Live events don't have a second take

From 2009 to 2013 I worked freelance in client relations and event coordination with Encompass Event Group and Wizcraft International Entertainment. The work included guest relations in the commentators' hospitality box at Chepauk for Chennai Super Kings during the IPL, the IPL Season 5 opening ceremony, the 100 Years of Indian Cinema celebration, promotions in Chennai for India's first Formula 1 Grand Prix, and the first season of the Celebrity Cricket League.

I didn't do it for the glamour. I worked those weekends and evenings to pay my own way through college instead of asking my mother for everything. She was the only earner in our family, and I wanted to carry my share. And I really loved the work.

On peak IPL match days I led a **40-strong hospitality crew**. And live events taught me lessons that apply directly to delivery:

- **The deadline is real.** The show starts on time whether you're ready or not.
- **Plans change constantly.** Guest lists shift, equipment fails, schedules move. What matters is how fast you adapt.
- **Communication is the job.** When something goes wrong backstage, the audience should never know.

Years later, in November 2020, I earned an event diagramming certification from Cvent, the event management platform. It had nothing to do with my job by then. I did it because it took me straight back to those days, and I said as much when I posted it.

## Healthcare claims and my first team

After graduating I joined Dell Technologies in 2013, working on healthcare claims for Conifer Health Solutions, then Blue Cross Blue Shield, Humana and Aetna. By 2014 I was a Senior Customer Care Representative leading a **15-member team**.

There I learned to manage with data. Using Excel models, Tableau and Salesforce, we tightened denial management, adding over **$100,000 a month** to collections, and as acting Operations Project Lead my team recovered more than **$150,000 a quarter** in payment discrepancies. We cut average call handling time by **33%** and lifted CSAT **above 90%**.

## Then technology

In July 2014 I moved into IT at Cognizant, and I've stayed in delivery ever since: production support, document platforms, Scrum, release trains, and now portfolio governance for AI-enabled products.

## The thread that connects it all

Whether it's a cricket stadium, a claims queue or an Agile Release Train, the work is the same at heart: get many people moving in the same direction, under pressure, toward a deadline that won't move. The tools changed. That part never did.

*If you value delivery leaders who've learned to perform under live pressure, I'd love to hear from you.*
