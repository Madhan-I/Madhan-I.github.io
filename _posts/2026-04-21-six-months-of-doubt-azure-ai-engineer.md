On July 1, 2021, I posted two words that had taken six months to earn: "nailed it". I'd passed the Azure AI Engineer exam.

The post that went with it was honest about the road there: "After six long months, having both doubts and uncertainty." It became one of the most-liked posts I've ever written. I think a lot of people recognized the doubt.

## How it started

At the end of 2020 Microsoft ran free workshops in India on Azure AI and data fundamentals. I took them, passed Azure AI Fundamentals on day 50 of my #100DaysOfCloud challenge, and followed it with Azure Data Fundamentals a few days later.

Fundamentals were the easy part. The engineer exam was a different level: designing and building real AI solutions with Azure's language, vision and machine learning services.

## The six months in between

Six months is a long time to carry an exam around, and I was honest at the time that doubt and uncertainty came with me the whole way.

What finally worked was practice tests. Not to memorize answers, but to find my missing pieces. Each wrong answer showed me a gap I hadn't known was there, and I went back to Microsoft Learn to fill it. By the time I took the exam, I knew where my gaps had been and had worked on each one.

## What happened next

The certificate was a beginning, not an end:

- I started teaching what I'd learned, from a webinar to help people prepare for the AI-900 exam in March 2022 to a guest lecture on Azure Custom Vision at VIT Chennai in April 2023.
- I renewed the certification every year, reaching my fourth year in 2023.
- Most importantly, it gave me the confidence to take on AI delivery at work.

## Why it matters for my day job

Today I lead delivery on AI-enabled platforms, including a compliance engine now in production. I'm not the person tuning the models, but I understand what the engineers are building, why the data matters and where the risks sit. That understanding started in those six months of doubt.

The other lesson is simpler. Hard things rarely feel ready. You find your gaps, close them one by one, and book the exam.
