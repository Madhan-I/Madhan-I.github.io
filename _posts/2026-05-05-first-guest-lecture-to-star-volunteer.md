On the last day of 2021 I wrote that one of the best things to happen to me that year was learning to speak in public. I had become a guest lecturer for universities and colleges, sharing what I knew with young people about to step into IT.

It didn't start on a stage. In my first year as a Microsoft Certified Trainer, from October 2020, I taught where I already was, at Atos Syntel:

- **My technology tower.** I ran several sessions for our Enterprise Content Management (ECM) group, a technology tower of more than 300 people, including sessions on the basics of AI and machine learning.
- **The wider office.** Through the company's learning department I also trained colleagues from across the office.

Teaching colleagues first gave me the practice and the confidence to take it outside.

## Into the universities

In September 2021 I gave my first university guest lecture, on Microsoft Azure Conversational AI, at the Vellore Institute of Technology. Technical videos from experienced trainers in the community inspired me to take the leap, and Microsoft Learn gave students a way to keep going after the session. It was the first of many.

## It snowballed

Over the next few months one session led to the next:

- **November 2021:** a virtual workshop for Sri Ramakrishna Engineering College on image classification and object detection with Azure Cognitive Services.
- **November 2021:** a careers session for final and third-year students, organised by the Rotaract clubs of several colleges, on what service-based IT companies look for and how to build a job profile.
- **January 2022:** a guest lecture at Jeppiaar Engineering College on machine learning and AI innovation in Azure, with an AutoML demo.
- **January 2022:** Google Cloud basics for college students, through Atos's CSR programme with EduBridge India.

## The session that doubled

Later that January I ran an AWS session for students with the NIIT Foundation, also through Atos CSR. It was planned as one hour. More than 110 people signed up, so we turned it into two parts and finished the following week.

That's still one of my favourite teaching moments. Nobody had to be there. They came because they wanted to learn cloud, and the demand changed the plan.

## The good things along the way

**Recognition I didn't expect.** In February 2022 Atos gave me a Spot award for the cloud training I'd run for students with NIIT Foundation and EduBridge India. The feedback from the students was good too.

**A day I won't forget.** In March 2022, being part of the CSR team gave me the chance to meet Atos executives in person. I called it one of the most memorable days of my life, and it was.

**Star Volunteer.** In March 2023 Atos named me Star Volunteer. I wrote then, and still believe, that sharing what I've learned with students has always helped me learn more myself.

## Why it matters for my day job

Teaching students is the best training I've had for leading delivery. Students tell you immediately when you're not making sense. That pushes you to explain the why before the how, which is exactly what a release train needs when it's planning across ten teams.

I've given more than 75 guest lectures since that first one at VIT. Every one of them still teaches me something.
