HEAT, the Home Energy Analysis Tool, was one of four Code for Boston projects I supported as a volunteer member during my Boston years. I was part of it from May 2023, when the project started.

## The project

HEAT is a live tool for Heat Smart Alliance energy coaches and homeowners. It estimates how much heat a home needs, using past gas bills and local weather data, then checks whether a proposed heat pump is big enough. It replaces a manual spreadsheet that coaches used to work through by hand.

## The tech

A Remix and React web app built on the Epic Stack, with a Python rules engine that runs in the browser to do the heat calculations. The app is deployed on Fly.io.

## How I supported it

I was on the non-technical side. I helped first-time contributors get the stack, Docker and their accounts running, so they could start on real issues quickly instead of losing their first evening to setup.

## What I learned

**Turning a spreadsheet into a product is real delivery work.** Every formula in the old spreadsheet had to be understood, tested and rebuilt. It's the same discipline as any migration I've led at work.

**Good tools need patient onboarding.** A modern stack is powerful but has many moving parts. Newcomers who had help in the first hour stayed.

## Why it matters for my day job

At work I've replaced manual processes with proper systems, from automating Walmart's reports to consolidating insurance templates. HEAT was the same story in a community setting.
