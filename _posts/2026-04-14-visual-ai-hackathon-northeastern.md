On 21 March 2025 I spent the day at Northeastern University in Boston, at one of Voxel51's Visual AI Hackathons. Voxel51 makes FiftyOne, an open-source toolkit for working with image and video datasets, and that spring it ran a series of these hackathons at universities across the US. The format was simple: tech talks, hands-on workshops, challenges to work on, and plenty of time to meet people.

## Why a delivery lead goes to a hackathon

I run release trains for a living, not computer-vision models. That's exactly why I went, and why I wanted to build rather than watch.

At work, AI arrives as a roadmap item: a compliance engine, a new model in production, a risk to manage. At a hackathon you see the other side. People take a dataset, a tool and a few hours, and find out what actually works. It's the fastest way I know to understand what engineers mean when they say something is "nearly done".

## What we built

My team's project combined image mapping with sentiment analysis: working out what an image shows, and the feeling it carries. I built part of it hands-on, which was a very different seat from the one I usually sit in, planning and governing the work of others.

Doing the work myself, even for a day, was the point. It reminded me how much effort hides behind a single line on a roadmap like "add sentiment scoring".

## What I took away

**Most of the work is the data.** Voxel51's whole approach, and the reason FiftyOne exists, is to look closely at your data before you trust your model. That's a useful reminder for anyone who plans AI delivery. The model is rarely the long pole; preparing, checking and labelling data usually is.

**Short time boxes sharpen decisions.** A one-day challenge forces teams to cut scope early and show something real. It's a sprint at full speed, and it shows how quickly people drop "nice to have" when the clock is visible.

**Mixed rooms solve problems faster.** A university hackathon puts students and working professionals in the same room. Fresh eyes ask the simple questions that experts skip, and those questions often unblock a team.

## The part I didn't expect

I went for the technology and came away with friends. A few of the people I met that day stayed in touch, and we still talk today, about work, careers and life. In a city where I was a long way from home, that mattered more than any prize.

## Why it matters for my day job

Leading AI programmes well means respecting how the work really gets done. Days like this one keep my plans honest, and they keep me connected to the people who do the building.
